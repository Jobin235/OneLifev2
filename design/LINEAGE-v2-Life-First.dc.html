TAKE 2 · LIFE FIRST
One Life
A life simulator where something is always happening to you. You read it, you choose, you live with it, you age up. The world is real underneath — you just never have to look at it.
WHAT I THREW OUT
✕  The dark trading-terminal look
✕  The World tab as a place you visit
✕  Visible world ticks and player badges
✕  Screens that read like database tables
✓  One screen: your life, right now, and a choice
🎂
The loop is the product
Something happens → you choose → you see what it cost you → Age Up. Every other screen is reachable in one tap and optional.
🚪
The world knocks, you don't visit
There is no World tab. A world change reaches you only as an ordinary life event, and only if you own something it touches.
🙈
The engine stays hidden
No ticks, no relevance scores, no "player-run" tags. Dates read as dates. Other players are just where some of the world's news quietly comes from.
😊
Warm, not cute
Paper cream, rounded cards, chunky type, five colour-coded life areas. Playful enough to be a game; calm enough to read a real story in.

4CThe end, and the next one
🕯️
Alex Rivera
1998 – 2081 · 83 years · Portland
Died in his sleep, at home, with the house paid off and the company still running.
THE WHOLE STORY
0–17
Portland, in a small house
Two parents, one income. Made the second soccer team at 15 and took a spot that was Marcus Webb's. Met Emma Chen in 10th grade math.
18–29
Work, and one big refusal
Computer science at Portland State, $31,000 of debt, cleared by 31. Joined Northgate Labs at 22. Turned down the Denver promotion at 26 because of Emma and never told her. Married her at 29.
30–41
One van, then six, then forty
Left Northgate at 32 and bought a van. Maya born at 31, Theo at 34. Survived the fuel spike at 34 by raising rates and losing a client. Bought his parents' house at 36. Diagnosed with high blood pressure at 38 and mostly ignored it.
42–58
The years that made his name
Won the Ward 4 council seat at 44 and served two terms. Pushed through the freight-idling ordinance at 49 — his one law. Refused to sell the company to a national carrier at 52. Stopped speaking to Theo at 55 over the succession.
59–83
Handing it over, badly and then well
Gave Maya the company at 61. Funded the Rivera Scholarship at Lincoln High at 64 — 39 kids so far. Heart attack at 71, survived it, walked four miles a day after. Emma died at 79. He lasted four more years.
HOW PEOPLE SAW HIM

Portland, Ward 4Respected, not liked

Everyone elseNever heard of him
He was never famous. Most people aren't.
WHAT HE ACTUALLY CHANGED
💼412 people drew a wage from his company over 51 years. Nine of them started their own.
⚖️The freight-idling ordinance is still law in Portland. It is the only thing he did that a stranger might benefit from today.
🎓The Rivera Scholarship has paid for 39 kids so far and is funded for another 60 years.
🏠He kept his parents' house in the family. His granddaughter lives in it now.
🕳️And one thing he never fixed: he stopped speaking to his son for 28 years and died before either of them moved.
